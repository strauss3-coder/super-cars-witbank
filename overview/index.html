01

Introduction

The first place a buyer meets you

Almost nobody walks onto a dealership floor cold any more. They look first, on a phone, usually in the evening, usually comparing you against three other dealers at the same time. This website is built to win that comparison.

The old problem with a dealership website is that it makes a buyer work. Stock is out of date, photographs are missing, the price is "call for details", and the phone number is three taps away. Every one of those is a reason to close the tab and open a competitor's.

This site removes those reasons one at a time. Stock is current. Prices are shown. A buyer can work out a monthly instalment themselves, then reach you on WhatsApp without typing a number — while they are still looking at the car that prompted it.

Built for the phone
Most of your buyers will never see this site on a computer. It was designed at phone width first and widened from there — not shrunk down from a desktop layout.
Fast on a weak signal
Pages carry no heavy frameworks and no advertising trackers. The site opens quickly on the kind of connection people actually have in Mpumalanga, not the kind agencies test on.
Reads as established
Twenty-five years of trading is stated plainly, alongside a verified 4.8 from 37 Google reviews. A buyer sizing you up finds the reassurance without hunting for it.
Always current
Stock, prices, photographs, opening hours and page copy are all yours to change from your own screen, and a change is live on the site immediately.

Everything on this site was written for Super Cars Witbank. There is no filler copy, no stock photography of somebody else's dealership, and no invented customer. The photographs are your floor, the address is your address, and the rating shown is the one you earned.

What the important ones do

Not every band carries the same weight. Five of them do most of the work, and they are worth understanding on their own terms.

Opening panel
What they see: a photograph of your forecourt, the line Quality pre-owned vehicles, and a search box. Why it exists: a visitor decides in about two seconds whether this is a real dealership. What it does for you: it answers that, and puts a search in front of them before they have scrolled once.
Featured stock
What they see: hand-picked vehicles from the current floor. Why it exists: most visitors want to see cars, not read about you. What it does for you: you choose what leads — the car you most want moved, not whatever happens to be newest.
Latest arrivals
What they see: the newest stock, with a Just in marker on anything recent. Why it exists: returning buyers check for what has changed. What it does for you: it rewards them for coming back, without you maintaining anything.
Finance made simple
What they see: a plain explanation and a route to the calculator. Why it exists: affordability is the question behind most hesitation. What it does for you: a buyer who has worked out a monthly figure arrives at your desk already halfway to a decision.
Contact section
What they see: phone, WhatsApp, email, today's opening hours and the map. Why it exists: the visitor who scrolled the whole page is the one most likely to act. What it does for you: it catches them at exactly that point, rather than sending them looking.
03

Browsing the floor

Finding the right car in under a minute

A buyer arrives with a shape in mind — a bakkie under three hundred, an automatic for the wife, something with service history. The stock page is built to get them from that vague idea to three real cars quickly.

Five ways to filter
Make, body type, fuel, transmission and availability. Choosing a make adds a model filter beneath it, so someone who picks Toyota is then offered Hilux, Fortuner and Corolla Cross rather than every model you have ever stocked.
Search that forgives
Typing fortuner, bakkie or diesel auto all work. A buyer does not have to know how you named the listing.
Results update instantly
No reloading, no waiting, no losing your place. The count updates as filters are chosen, so a buyer can see straight away whether they have narrowed it too far.
The vehicle card
Photograph, year, make, model, variant, mileage, transmission, fuel, price and an estimated monthly figure. Enough to shortlist without opening anything.
Price badges
A vehicle can carry a market marker — Great Price, Fair Price — so a buyer comparing four dealers can see where yours sits. Badges are yours to set or clear per vehicle.
Availability is honest
Reserved and sold vehicles are marked as such rather than quietly removed. A floor that visibly moves stock is a floor that is trading.
Saved vehicles
A buyer can keep a shortlist on their own device and come back to it. Nothing is asked of them — no account, no email address.
Side-by-side comparison
Two or three vehicles can be put next to each other on one screen, with the better figure in each row marked. It settles the "which one" question on your site instead of on someone else's.
04

The vehicle page

Where the decision is actually made

Everything before this page is navigation. This is the page a buyer sits on, sends to their partner, and comes back to twice before phoning. It carries every fact they need and four ways to act on it.

Full gallery
Every photograph of the vehicle, at a size worth looking at, with thumbnails beneath and swipe on a phone.
Specification
Model year, mileage, transmission, fuel, body, colour, doors, seats, engine power, fuel consumption and emissions — each shown only when you have captured it. A blank is never filled with a guess.
Service history
Stated openly where it exists, alongside overall condition. The single most common question about a used car, answered before it is asked.
Description and features
Your own words about the car, and a list of what it is fitted with.
Price and monthly estimate
The asking price, and beneath it an indicative instalment so affordability is answered on the same screen.
Four ways to act
WhatsApp opens a message already naming the vehicle. Call dials you directly from a phone. Enquire sends a written question. Book a test drive asks for a time. A buyer picks whichever suits their temperament.
Related vehicles
Similar stock beneath, so a buyer who has ruled this one out stays on your floor instead of returning to a search engine.

The WhatsApp message matters more than it looks. It arrives already saying which vehicle, which stock number and which price — so the first thing you type is an answer, not a question. Fewer messages, faster replies, and no confusion about which car is being asked about.

05

Finance

Answering "what will it cost me a month?"

This is the question that decides most used car sales, and the one buyers are least comfortable phoning to ask. The calculator lets them answer it themselves, in private, before they speak to anyone.

Vehicle price
Filled in automatically when the buyer arrives from a vehicle page, or typed in directly.
Deposit
A slider from nothing to half the price, showing the rand amount alongside the percentage as it moves.
Repayment term
Twelve months through eighty-four, chosen from real bank terms rather than typed.
Interest rate
Adjustable, starting from the rate you set. When the prime rate moves, you change it once and every calculation on the site follows.
Balloon payment
Optional, for buyers who want the lower monthly figure and understand the trade-off.
The result
A monthly instalment with the initiation and service fees included, so the figure a buyer sees is the figure they will be quoted — not one that grows at your desk.
Application
From the result, a buyer can apply straight away. The application reaches you with everything the bank will ask for already gathered.

Why pre-approval helps you

A buyer who applies before visiting arrives already knowing what they can spend. That turns a browsing conversation into a buying one, and it stops you spending an afternoon on somebody the bank was never going to approve. The page states plainly that you deal with ABSA Vehicle Finance and the other major banks, which is reassurance a first-time buyer needs to hear.

06

Sell or trade in

Stock coming in, not just going out

Good used stock is harder to buy than to sell. This page turns your website into a source of it, and every submission arrives with enough detail to put a number to the car without a phone call first.

They send the details

Make, model, year, mileage and a few photographs, uploaded from the phone they took them on.

You value it

The submission reaches you complete. You check the market and the condition, then put a number to it.

They decide

Take the offer as cash, or put it towards a car on your floor. The second outcome is the valuable one.

The page is deliberately short. Every extra field on a valuation form costs submissions, and a seller who has to dig out a VIN before they can ask a question usually does not come back. What is asked for is what you need to value the car — nothing more.

Reviews

Proof that comes from somewhere else

A review a dealer writes about themselves is worth nothing, and buyers know it. What carries weight is a rating from a source the buyer already trusts — which is why the reviews page leads with the platforms rather than with quotes.

Your Google rating
4.8 out of 5, from 37 reviews, shown prominently with a link through to read every one of them on Google. The link matters: a rating a visitor can go and verify is worth more than one they cannot.
Your AutoTrader rating
4.4 out of 5, from 14 dealer reviews, shown alongside it. Two independent sources agreeing is stronger than one.
Written reviews
The page has a place for individual reviews to be shown in full, with the customer's name and the vehicle they bought. These are yours to add as you gather permission to publish them.
On the home page too
The rating appears in the opening panel and again further down, so a visitor who never reaches the reviews page still sees it.

No customer has been invented for this site. Where written reviews are not yet published, the page says so and sends the visitor to Google to read the real ones — which is both honest and, for a dealer with a 4.8, the more persuasive option.

10

On a phone

Where most of this will actually be read

Assume a buyer on a phone, one-handed, on a patchy signal, in the evening. That is the real condition this site was designed for — not a designer's monitor.

One layout, every size
The same site adapts from a small phone to a wide monitor. There is no separate mobile version to fall out of date.
Built for thumbs
Every button, filter and menu control is at least the size Apple and Google both specify as the minimum a thumb can hit reliably. This was measured across all 655 controls on the site, not estimated.
A menu that opens first time
One tap opens it, one tap closes it, and a tap anywhere else dismisses it. Tested to a hundred consecutive cycles without a miss.
Galleries you swipe
Vehicle photographs move the way every other application on the phone moves them.
Filters that do not take over
On a phone the filters open as a panel over the results and close when the buyer is done, rather than pushing the cars off the screen.
Calculator sliders
Deposit and rate are dragged, not typed. Nobody types a percentage into a phone keyboard by choice.
Tap to call, tap to message
Numbers dial. WhatsApp opens. Addresses open the maps application. Nothing has to be copied out by hand.

How it feels to use

The part nobody describes but everybody notices

A buyer will never say "the transitions were well judged". They will say it felt like a proper company. That impression is made of a dozen small decisions, and it transfers directly onto how they judge your cars.

Content arrives settled
Sections ease into place slightly before they are scrolled to, so by the time the buyer's eye reaches them they have already arrived. Nothing is ever waited for.
One motion language
Every animation on the site runs at one of three durations and on one of three curves. Consistency is what separates considered from decorated.
Nothing jumps
Space is reserved for every photograph before it loads, so the page never shifts under a thumb that is already reaching for a button.
Immediate feedback
Buttons respond at the point of contact. Forms say what they are doing while they do it, and confirm plainly when they are done.
Photographs fade in
An image appears once it has actually loaded rather than snapping into place half-drawn.
Motion can be switched off
A visitor whose device asks for reduced motion gets the site with the movement removed and nothing else lost.
Reachable by keyboard
Every control can be reached and operated without a mouse, and every one announces itself properly to a screen reader.

The site was measured as well as designed: page-load work audited, duplicated scroll handling removed, images sized to what they are actually drawn at, and every interactive element on every page checked for a name, a reachable focus state and a large enough target.
